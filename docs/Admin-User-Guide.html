1. Getting started

What this is: the admin panel is the control room of your website. From here you manage every page, every booking, and the WhatsApp assistant — no coding needed.
  1. Open your admin web address in a browser and log in with your email and password.
  2. On the left is the menu (sidebar) — every section of the site is a menu item.
  3. Click a menu item to open that section. Most sections show a list; click Add to create, or the Edit button to change an existing item.
  4. After editing, always click Save / Update. Changes appear on the live website immediately.
📷 Screenshot: the admin login screen & the left-hand menu (sidebar)
Golden rule: you can only see the menu items your account has permission for. If something is missing, an administrator can grant you access (see section 12).

2. Dashboard

What this is: the first screen after login — a quick snapshot of what's happening.

It shows counts and today's appointments at a glance (owner, pet, consultation type, status). It's read-only — think of it as your morning overview. Click any item to open its full details.

How to update any website section (example: a doctor)

4. Appointments

What this is: every appointment request — whether booked on the website or through WhatsApp — lands here for your team to manage.

How a booking flows

A pet parent books (website form or WhatsApp) → the appointment is created automatically → it appears under Appointments → the pet parent gets a confirmation SMS/email → your team updates the status (e.g. Confirmed, Rescheduled, Completed).

The sub-sections

  • Appointments — all appointments booked on the website.
  • WhatsApp Appointments — all appointments booked via the chatbot (same details, separate tab).
  • Appointment Users — the pet parents' profiles (built automatically when they book/log in).
  • Manage Statuses — the list of statuses you can set (Pending, Confirmed, Rescheduled, Cancelled, etc.). Some statuses can auto-send an SMS to the pet parent.

How to handle an appointment

  1. Open Appointments (or WhatsApp Appointments).
  2. Use the filters (date, status, search) to find one.
  3. Click View to see full details (pet, owner, speciality, reason).
  4. Change the Status and save. If that status has an SMS set up (like "Rescheduled" or "Cancelled"), the pet parent is notified automatically.

5. The WhatsApp Chatbot

What this is: an automatic assistant on WhatsApp. When a pet parent messages your number, the bot replies with a menu and guides them — book an appointment, see services, timings, request reports, or talk to your team.

What the pet parent experiences

Pet parent sends "Hi" → bot shows the main menu → they pick an option (e.g. Book appointment) → the bot asks a few friendly questions → they confirm → an appointment is created and a confirmation SMS is sent → when they say "thank you", the chat ends politely.

The menu options

  • Book appointment — asks if they're a new or existing client, collects the needed details, speciality, day & time, then books it.
  • Our services — a tappable list of your key services and specialities.
  • Timings & location — hours, address and directions.
  • Emergency help — the emergency number and address.
  • Medical reports — collects a few details so your team can share the pet's reports.
  • Talk to our team — captures their message for your team to follow up.
Good to know: the bot only replies after the pet parent messages first (a WhatsApp rule). Everything it says — the questions, options, menu and answers — can be edited by you in Chatbot Mgmt (next section).

6. Chatbot Management

What this is: the section (menu item Chatbot Mgmt) where you control everything the chatbot says — no developer needed.

What you can change

  • Any question or message the bot sends (reword it however you like).
  • The answer options shown to pet parents, and their order (drag to reorder).
  • The main menu items and the welcome greeting.
  • Info screens — timings, emergency text — and FAQ answers.
  • Add brand-new items — your own extra questions, new FAQs, or new menu buttons.

How to edit a chatbot message

  1. Open Chatbot Mgmt. You'll see items grouped by section (Menu, Booking, Info, FAQ…).
  2. Click Edit on the item you want to change.
  3. Edit the message in the WhatsApp-style editor — a live preview shows exactly how it'll look.
  4. For option lists, drag the ↕ handle to reorder, tick/untick to show/hide.
  5. Click Save changes.
📷 Screenshot: the Chatbot Mgmt edit screen with the message editor & live preview

How to add a new item

  1. Click + Add new item.
  2. Choose where it goes (a booking question, a medical-report question, an FAQ, or a main-menu option).
  3. Type the text (and options if it's multiple-choice), set the priority/order, and Save.
Note: the order of the built-in questions and the conversation's branching are fixed in code (to keep the flow working safely). You can freely edit wording, options, FAQs, menu items, and add your own questions. For a change to the fixed step order, ask your developer — it's a quick change.

8. Medical Report Requests

What this is: when a pet parent uses the chatbot's "Medical reports" option, their request (client name, pet name, patient ID, mobile, report needed) appears here.
  1. Open Form Enquiries → Medical Report Requests.
  2. Click View to see the details.
  3. Use Reply on WhatsApp or Call to share the report with the pet parent.

9. Reports

What this is: summaries and downloadable lists to understand your activity.
  • Overview — the big picture (totals and trends).
  • Appointments — a filterable, downloadable list of all appointments.
  • Operational — day-to-day operational figures.
  • Clients — your pet-parent base.
  • Communication — a log of every SMS and email the system sent, with sent/failed status.
Handy check: if someone says they didn't get a confirmation SMS, open Reports → Communication to see whether it was sent or failed.

10. Notifications

What this is: the live bell at the top-right. It alerts your team the moment a new appointment, report request, or enquiry comes in.

Click the bell to see recent notifications; click one to jump straight to that record. It updates on its own — no need to refresh.

11. Activity & Communication Logs

  • Activity Log — a history of what was changed in the panel and by whom (useful for accountability).
  • Communication Logs — the detailed record of every SMS/email attempt (same data the Communication report summarises).

12. Users, Roles & Permissions

What this is: who can log in and what they're allowed to see/do. (Usually managed by a Super Admin.)
  • Users — the staff accounts that can log into the panel.
  • Roles — groups like "Admin" or "Front Office" that bundle a set of permissions.
  • Permissions — the fine-grained access (view/create/edit/delete) for each module.

To give a staff member access

  1. Create their account under Users and assign a Role (e.g. Front Office).
  2. The role decides which menu items they'll see. Adjust the role's permissions if needed.

13. The public website (what visitors see)

What this is: the parts your pet parents interact with on the live website. You don't manage these day-to-day, but it helps to know how they work.

Booking an appointment online

Visitor clicks Book Appointment → enters mobile number → gets an OTP (one-time code) by SMS → verifies → fills the booking form (pet & visit details) → ticks the "I'm not a robot" box → submits → the appointment appears in your Appointments list and they receive a confirmation.
📷 Screenshot: the online Book Appointment form

Contact form

The Contact page form also has the "I'm not a robot" check to stop spam. Genuine messages arrive under Form Enquiries → Contact Enquiries, and your team is alerted by the bell.

"Chat with us" button

The green Chat with us button (and the WhatsApp icon) in the website header open a WhatsApp chat with your hospital number, pre-filled with "Hi" — which starts the chatbot. All of these point to the same number.

📷 Screenshot: the "Chat with us" button in the website header
Spam protection ("I'm not a robot"): this is handled automatically. If you ever change the website address (domain), the robot-check needs the new address registered with Google — that's a quick one-time task for your developer.
